# BOLDGenotyper 2.2.0: recommend the threshold where haplotypes settle, not the elbow

**Status (2026-10-20): done on `release/v2.2.0`, awaiting review before the tag.**

**Why.** `boldgenotyper-sweep` recommends the elbow of the haplotype-count curve: the
largest absolute second difference (`find_elbow_point`). These curves decay convexly (each
step removes fewer haplotypes than the one before), so the second difference is largest at
the second threshold tested, wherever the range starts. On six BOLD downloads (an order, two
families, three genera), sweeps starting at 0.000, 0.005 and 0.010 put the elbow at 0.005,
0.010 and 0.015 in five of the six. Kneedle (largest distance below the chord) moves with the
start too. The recommendation reported the range, not the data.

**Change.**
- `find_settled_threshold(rates, max_pct_changed=1.0)`: the smallest threshold from which
  every later step between adjacent thresholds changes the haplotype of fewer than
  `max_pct_changed` percent of records (the stability table, `adjacent_change_rates`). It
  compares neighbors only, so it does not depend on where the range starts. The share is
  taken unrounded (`n_changed / n_records`), not from the rounded `pct_changed`. None when
  no threshold settles, or without the tracking table.
- The sweep recommends the settled threshold: `recommendations.txt` marks it `<- settled`
  and uses it in the suggested command; `results['recommended_threshold']`, the sweep's
  README, the CLI summary and `elbow_plot.pdf` show it.
- The elbow is still reported (`<- elbow`, `results['elbow_threshold']`, a gray line in the
  plot), for reference, with a caution that it depends on the range tested and, when it is
  the second threshold tested, that it is there because of the range.
- Default `--thresholds`: 0.005 to 0.030 in steps of 0.005 (Steph, 20 Oct 2026; were 0.01,
  0.015, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, uneven and starting above the pipeline's default 0.005).
- Notes in `recommendations.txt`: when the first threshold is already settled (sweep lower
  values to see where it settles); when no threshold settles (extend the range upward); when
  thresholds are unevenly spaced (a wider step changes more records).

**Calls made here (for Steph's review).**
- The cutoff (1%) is a module constant and a keyword argument of `generate_recommendations`
  and `run_parameter_sweep`, with no CLI flag: the stability table shows every step, so a
  reader can apply another cutoff from it.
- The elbow is kept and `elbow_plot.pdf` keeps its name, so scripts that read either still
  work.
- Pipeline results are unchanged: only the sweep's recommendation and its text and plot.

**Tests** (`tests/test_sweep_recommendations.py`): the settled threshold on a table with a
late rise above the cutoff; unchanged when the first threshold is dropped (the elbow moves);
unrounded share; None when nothing settles; the recommendation text with both marks and the
notes; the sweep workflow end to end with the pipeline faked (results, README); the CLI
summary.

**Checks.**
- Full suite: 553 passed, 8 skipped, 0 failed (2.1.1: 541 passed; 12 new tests). Sphinx
  build: no warnings.
- `recommendations.txt` regenerated from saved sweeps of three of the six datasets
  (0.000-0.080, step 0.005): settled 0.005, 0.010 (the shared order-level dataset, whose
  sweep starts at 0.010, so the "sweep lower values" note appears) and 0.015, where the
  elbow was 0.005, 0.015 and 0.005.
- Simulations with known haplotypes (15 saved sweeps from 0.005, 5 replicates per
  scenario): the elbow was 0.010, the second threshold, in all 15. The settled threshold
  was 0.010 for one species (recall 0.85, precision 1.00, as at the elbow), 0.010-0.020 at
  family level (recall 0.87 against 0.93 at the elbow; 0.8 against 0.6 species lost), and
  for one sequence per haplotype without the 2.0.0 exemption mostly 0.015 (recall 0.16 as
  at the elbow; precision 1.00 against 0.86). With the exemption it was 0.005 (4 of 5),
  scores unchanged.
